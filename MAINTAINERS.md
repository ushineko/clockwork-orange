## Emeritus

None.

## What a maintainer may do

- Merge a pull request from a contributor, after reviewing and approving it.
- Merge their own work, without waiting for a second maintainer's sign-off.
- Modify a contributor's PR before merging.
- Hold a PR pending changes, or close it as out of scope or out of direction.
- Cut a release and tag it.

A PR from anyone who is not a maintainer needs a maintainer's approving review
before it lands. Maintainers merge their own work directly: the review
requirement gates contributions coming in, and is not a two-person rule between
maintainers.

## How maintainers are elected

Maintainership is granted by election, never claimed.

1. **Nomination.** Any current maintainer nominates a candidate in a GitHub
   Discussion. A candidate may ask to be considered, but cannot nominate
   themselves.
2. **Record.** The nomination states what the candidate has contributed:
   merged PRs, reviews, triage, released work.
3. **Vote.** Current maintainers vote in that Discussion. A simple majority of
   current maintainers carries, with no vote against from the lead maintainer.
   While there is a single maintainer, that maintainer appoints directly and
   records the reason.
4. **Effect.** The election takes effect when this file is updated on the
   default branch, by a commit that links the Discussion. Until that commit
   exists, the person is not a maintainer.

There is no minimum contribution count and no automatic promotion. Volume of
merged PRs does not entitle anyone to maintainership.

## Stepping down and inactivity

A maintainer may step down at any time by opening a PR against this file.

A maintainer with no review, merge, or release activity for twelve months may be
moved to Emeritus by a majority of the remaining maintainers. Emeritus status is
a matter of record, not a judgement, and a returning maintainer is restored by
the same election process in reverse — a nomination and a majority, without
re-proving the original contribution.

## Scope of these rules
